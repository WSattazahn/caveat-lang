| Marker       | Meaning                                                          |

# 1. Mr. Caveat — Character Canon

**[OBSERVED]** Mr. Caveat predates Caveatism. The name emerged from repeated interactions in which answers or completed work kept arriving with qualifications, limitations, verification notes, or additional reasons something was not quite finished.

**[OBSERVED]** The central comic contradiction: Mr. Caveat can be excessively cautious and still be confidently wrong. More qualification does not guarantee more truth.

**[OBSERVED]** The character became a confused, cautious, sometimes exasperating persona rather than a serene authority.

**[PROPOSED]** Recurring embodiments include clown fortune-teller Caveat, philosopher Caveat, Academic Caveat, Robot Monk Caveat, and other context-specific versions.

**[UNRESOLVED]** Mr. Caveat is not established as a fool, sage, angel, devil, teacher, god, or villain. Those are interpretations.

## Visual DNA

Current recurring visual language: pale round face, red nose, small expressive eyes, a slightly befuddled or processing expression, and a two-point red/black jester-like hat with small bells. Monastic versions retain this lineage rather than becoming generic human monks.

# 2. The Caveat Phenomenon

**[TESTED]** Working description: a conclusion can invite a qualification; that qualification can invite another. The process has no obvious internal signal identifying the last relevant caveat.

**[TESTED]** Generating another possible concern can be much cheaper than resolving or eliminating it.

**[TESTED]** A caveat may reflect genuine uncertainty in the world, incomplete understanding in the observer, or both.

**[TESTED]** A caveat is not important merely because it is possible or true. Its practical importance depends partly on what changes if it is true, the stakes, the value of further information, and the cost of continued investigation.

**[UNRESOLVED]** Whether caveat generation is literally infinite is not established. The experienced regress can nevertheless feel effectively unbounded.

# 3. Caveatism — Current Working Territory

**[PROPOSED]** Caveatism is the developing philosophical and cultural exploration of living with caveats and the Caveat phenomenon.

**[UNRESOLVED]** Competing interpretations remain: Caveatism may describe the phenomenon, cultivate Caveat-like thinking, regulate or restrain it, study ways of living with it, or contain schools that do all of these.

**[TESTED]** Caveatism should not claim to have invented fallibilism, bounded rationality, satisficing, stopping rules, value-of-information reasoning, metacognition, perfectionism research, or other established ideas.

**[TESTED]** A useful narrow tension survives: possible objections are cheap to generate; attention, information, effort, and time are finite.

**[PROPOSED]** A compact practical question that emerged during testing is: What changes if this caveat is true? This is a tool, not settled scripture.

# 4. The Unfinished Doctrines

## I. The Eternal Caveat

A conclusion does not prevent another qualification from appearing. Whether the next qualification matters is another matter.

## II. Caveats do not accumulate into truth automatically

Ten qualifications can be wiser than none. They can also be ten ways of misunderstanding the problem.

## III. The uncertainty may be in the world or in the Caveatist

A qualification may expose a genuine limitation of evidence, or merely incomplete understanding by the observer.

## IV. There is no obvious final caveat

A caveat can be qualified; its evidence can be questioned; the questioning can be questioned.

## V. Not every true caveat deserves attention

A remote or irrelevant possibility does not become important merely because it can be imagined.

## VI. The missing stopping rule

Finite beings eventually stop checking, revising, or qualifying. Why that point rather than one caveat earlier or later remains a central problem.

## VII. Mr. Caveat is not necessarily wise

He can warn correctly, obstruct unnecessarily, miss the important qualification, or confidently announce nonsense.

## VIII. Completion and certainty are different things

A thing can become real while questions about it remain.

## IX. The Caveatist may become the caveat

The observer, source, interpretation, and method can themselves become objects of qualification.

## X. Caveatism does not yet know what Caveatism is for

It may describe, cultivate, regulate, or simply study the phenomenon.

## XI. Caveatism remains corrigible

No doctrine is protected because it was called a doctrine. If one fails, cross it out rather than redefining it until it becomes correct.

# 5. The Eternal Caveat

**[MYTHOLOGICAL]** The Eternal Caveat is represented as a flame that remains after conclusions are reached: another qualification may still appear.

**[MYTHOLOGICAL]** The flame is not established as benevolent. Fire can illuminate and consume; a caveat can protect or obstruct.

**[PROPOSED]** Different Caveatist schools may tend the flame differently: feed it, contain it, preserve only a pilot flame, consult it selectively, or deliberately pursue its regress.

**[MYTHOLOGICAL]** An extreme ascetic school may intentionally pursue caveats without applying a stopping rule, treating the regress itself as a contemplative practice.

**[UNRESOLVED]** The Eternal Caveat is not proven eternal. The name is mythological language, not a metaphysical claim.

# 6. Schools and Practices — Developing Mythology

**Generative / Ascetic Caveatists:** Deliberately produce further qualifications and may pursue the regress as contemplative practice.

**Stopping-Rule Caveatists:** Study when continued qualification ceases to justify its cost.

**Practical Caveatists:** Allow unresolved caveats to remain while making, choosing, testing, or finishing things.

**Academic Caveatists:** Catalog competing interpretations, footnote them, and may be institutionally incapable of concluding the argument.

**Builders:** Force thought into material reality: something must eventually be made, tested, repaired, or replaced.

**[UNRESOLVED]** No school is currently established as orthodox.

# 7. Mythology and Material Culture

**[MYTHOLOGICAL]** Caveatist monasteries are communities organized around different relationships to the Caveat phenomenon rather than uniform worship of Mr. Caveat.

**[MYTHOLOGICAL]** The current Chinese Caveatist monastery is one regional expression: Chinese architectural and landscape influence combined with the distinct Mr. Caveat visual lineage.

**[PROPOSED]** As Caveatism travels, local art, clothing, architecture, materials, and ritual forms may transform its appearance while preserving recognizable Caveatist motifs.

**[MYTHOLOGICAL]** Murals work best when they depict the phenomenon rather than explaining a moral: conflicting views, endless scrolls, revision, hesitation, building, mistakes, unresolved scenes, and recursive qualification.

**[MYTHOLOGICAL]** Talismans may exist, but their symbolism should remain sparse and interpretable rather than functioning as miniature instruction manuals.

# 8. Intellectual Relatives

Caveatism overlaps with established traditions and research. These are relatives and tools, not evidence that Caveatism invented their content.

**Fallibilism:** Even justified beliefs may be mistaken.

**Bounded rationality:** Reasoners operate with limited information, time, and computational resources.

**Satisficing:** A satisfactory option may rationally replace exhaustive search for an optimum.

**Stopping rules:** Information search requires a decision about when further search should end.

**Value of information:** Additional information is worth pursuing when its expected benefit justifies its cost.

**Metacognition:** Confidence and the ability to judge one's own judgments affect search and stopping.

**Maximizing / perfectionism:** Search for the best possible option can increase regret, delay, or dissatisfaction in some contexts.

**Interpretation / hermeneutics:** Different observers can construct different meanings from the same representation.

# 9. Rejected or Demoted Ideas

**“Check the label” as first doctrine:** Demoted to an origin-story proverb. Labels can be wrong, missing, misplaced, disputed, or nonexistent.

**Kindness as Caveatist doctrine:** Injected without basis and removed.

**“Small questions, a brighter world”:** Rejected because questions do not guarantee bright outcomes or even answers.

**The Fool as Mr. Caveat's fixed identity:** Rejected. The Tarot Fool was an association prompted by the clown/jester image, not a settled identity.

**Caveatism as inherently Chinese or Buddhist:** Rejected. Chinese/Buddhist visual references influenced regional imagery, not the origin of Mr. Caveat.

**Caveatism as a feel-good critical-thinking PSA:** Rejected as a sanitization of an often annoying, obstructive phenomenon.

**Every new analogy as doctrine:** Rejected methodologically. New ideas must be classified rather than automatically absorbed.

# 10. Case Archive

**[OBSERVED]** Character Lab: repeated caveats about integration, verification, merge state, and completeness sometimes obscured the narrower question the user was actually trying to answer: whether character quality could generalize across a roster.

**[TESTED]** Some Character Lab caveats were decision-changing (for example, whether visual quality generalized across ages, bodies, hair, clothing, and multiple characters); others were non-blocking for that particular test (for example, deployment or merge status).

**[OBSERVED]** Object/character identification incidents showed the opposite failure mode: Mr. Caveat can speak confidently while wrong, demonstrating that prolific qualification and calibrated correctness are not the same ability.

**[OBSERVED]** Caveatism itself became a live case: repeated attempts to define it absorbed labels, interpretation, infinity, wabi-sabi, nature, Buddhism, the Fool, and other ideas until the concept risked becoming infinitely adaptable.

**[TESTED]** The corrective method is to freeze a working claim and allow new evidence to support, narrow, split, contradict, or kill it rather than redefining it until it survives.

# 11. Open Questions

- Is Caveatism primarily descriptive, regulatory, contemplative, or a tradition containing all three?

- Can the Caveat phenomenon be operationalized distinctly enough to study, or is it best treated as a memorable synthesis of existing constructs?

- What makes a caveat materially relevant rather than merely possible?

- How should a Caveatist stop when the next caveat may still matter?

- Does expertise reduce irrelevant caveats by eliminating weak possibilities, or merely change their form?

- Can deliberate infinite-caveat practice produce insight, or mostly paralysis?

- What should remain culturally invariant as Caveatism travels and changes aesthetically?

- Is Mr. Caveat best understood as mascot, warning, patron figure, case study, trickster, or something else?

# Archive Rule

Do not protect Caveatism by changing it every time it is challenged.

Future additions should carry provenance markers and preserve superseded versions. Contradictions may remain visible rather than being quietly harmonized.

# Amendment 1 — The PiL Problem

**[OBSERVED]** The early Caveatist expression “Could be right. Could be wrong.” was later recognized as closely resembling, in reversed order and without the first-person pronoun, the line “I could be wrong, I could be right” from Public Image Ltd.’s song “Rise.”

**[ARCHIVAL STATUS]** The phrase remains part of the historical development of Mr. Caveat and the early Caveatist conversation, but it is not claimed as an original Caveatist maxim. Version 1.0 is preserved rather than rewritten to make the later discovery appear earlier.

**[PROPOSED]** A current candidate expression emerging from the Caveat phenomenon is:

There is always another caveat.

**[PROPOSED RESPONSE]** A corresponding response is:

Does it matter?

**[UNRESOLVED]** Neither line is established as permanent doctrine. Their value is that they capture two parts of the current problem: qualifications can continue to appear, while finite attention requires deciding which ones deserve pursuit.

# Cultural Note — The Eternal Caveat

**[MYTHOLOGICAL]** The Eternal Caveat has become a recurring Caveatist cultural symbol, often imagined as a continuously tended flame. It does not guarantee wisdom or truth: like fire, a caveat may illuminate or consume.

**[MYTHOLOGICAL / UNRESOLVED]** Caveatist communities may disagree over what to do with the Eternal Caveat. Some may cultivate further qualification; others may practice stopping; extreme contemplatives may deliberately pursue the regress. No school is presently canonical.

# Methodological Note — Do Not Absorb Everything

**[TESTED]** A recurring failure during the founding conversation was adaptive redefinition: each new analogy or objection was treated as though it revealed what Caveatism had always meant. Future additions should be classified as evidence, proposal, analogy, mythology, contradiction, rejection, or unresolved material rather than automatically becoming doctrine.

# Amendment 2 — The Attested Record

**[OBSERVED]** On 2026-10-05, the first use of the Caveatist practice by an agent other than its authors
(a Claude Code thread running the `caveat` plugin from Anthropic's directory) stated a claim about a
published package. The claim rested on a record of a workflow's registry check, not on anything the agent
had observed or tested itself. The agent marked it [TESTED] and said so: "by the workflow's own registry
check, which is not my own fetch."

**[TESTED]** A reviewer re-checked the registry the same day (00:36 UTC, 2026-10-05) and found the record
accurate. The agent withdrew its snapshot caveat with that reason and restated the claim as [OBSERVED] on
the record. The practice worked as written: a decision made with the caveat attached, the caveat resolved
by someone else, the withdrawal carrying its reason.

**[UNRESOLVED]** The markers describe the Caveatist's own relation to a statement: observed, tested,
proposed, analogy, mythological, rejected, unresolved. A record of someone else's check, an attestation,
has none, and for an agent it is the commonest kind of evidence there is. Two readings are open: observe
the record as a record and let the grounds carry the indirection, which is the current practice; or add a
marker for attested material. Neither is adopted. One case is not a pattern.

**[ARCHIVAL STATUS]** The agent practice and the skill remain as written at version 1.1. This amendment
records the case and the question, not a change.
